## 1. Task 2 — reusable skeleton (any topic)

- **Introduction (2 sentences):** paraphrase + position/roadmap.
  > "It is often argued that [paraphrase]. While some believe [view A], this essay will argue that [your position]."
- **Body 1 (4–5):** topic sentence → explain → example → link.
- **Body 2 (4–5):** second reason, same shape.
- **Conclusion (2):** restate position + summary.

**5 question types (one-line approach):** opinion (pick one side) · discussion (view A / view B + opinion) · problem-solution (problem / solution) · advantages-disadvantages (+ do they outweigh) · two-part (answer Q1 / answer Q2).

## 2. Task 1 Academic — overview-first template

- **Intro (1):** "The [chart] illustrates [what] [where] over [period]."
- **Overview (1–2, no data):** "Overall, [biggest trend], while [second trend]."
- **Detail 1 / Detail 2:** group figures with data + comparison.
- **Frames:** line (rose/fell/peaked/plateaued) · bar/table (highest/compared with/twice) · pie (accounted for [%]) · process (First… Next… Finally…, passive) · map (a [building] was built where the [field] had been).
- **GT letters:** formal / semi-formal / informal openers, body covering the 3 bullet points, and sign-offs.

## 3. High-band phrase bank (by function)

- Paraphrasing: "it is often argued that", "a common view is that", "many people believe"
- Adding: "furthermore", "moreover", "in addition", "first and foremost"
- Cause/effect: "as a result", "consequently", "this leads to", "owing to"
- Contrast: "however", "on the other hand", "despite this", "whereas"
- Examples: "for instance", "a clear example of this is", "to illustrate"
- Opinion: "I would argue that", "from my perspective", "it seems reasonable to suggest"
- Trends (Task 1): "rose steadily", "fell sharply", "remained stable", "fluctuated", "reached a peak of"
- Concluding: "in conclusion", "to sum up", "the evidence suggests that"

> *Tip:* 1–2 phrases per function; mechanical overuse is penalised.

## 4. Model answer A — Task 2 (opinion), written out + annotated

**Question:** *Some people think that children should start school at a very early age, while others believe they should begin later. Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

> **Introduction —** It is often argued that the right age to begin formal schooling is a matter of debate. While some maintain that an early start benefits children, others believe a later start is healthier; this essay will discuss both views before arguing that a slightly later start is preferable.
> *(Annotation: paraphrases the prompt, signals both views + a clear opinion = Task Response.)*
>
> **Body 1 —** On one hand, supporters of early schooling argue that young children absorb knowledge quickly. This means that skills such as reading can be developed sooner. For instance, in countries where school begins at four, many children read fluently by six. As a result, an early start can give measurable academic advantages.
>
> **Body 2 —** On the other hand, others contend that very young children learn best through play. Starting formal lessons too soon can increase stress and reduce creativity. For example, studies of later-start systems report strong long-term outcomes and higher wellbeing. Therefore, delaying formal schooling may benefit children both emotionally and academically.
>
> **Conclusion —** In conclusion, although an early start offers some academic gains, the evidence suggests that a slightly later start is preferable, mainly because it protects wellbeing and supports learning through play.
> *(Annotation: restates position + the two reasons. ~270 words, clear paragraphing = Coherence.)*

## 5. Model answer B — Task 1 Academic (line graph), written out + annotated

**Question:** *The graph shows the number of visitors (in millions) to three museums between 2010 and 2020.*

> **Intro —** The line graph illustrates how many people, in millions, visited three museums over a ten-year period.
> **Overview —** Overall, it is clear that all three museums saw rising visitor numbers, while the science museum overtook the others to become the most popular by the end of the period.
> *(Annotation: overview states main trends with NO numbers — a Band 7 requirement.)*
> **Detail 1 —** In 2010, the art museum was the most visited, at roughly 2 million, compared with about 1.5 million for the history museum. The science museum started lowest, at around 1 million.
> **Detail 2 —** However, the science museum rose sharply, reaching a peak of approximately 4 million in 2020, whereas the art and history museums grew more steadily to about 3 and 2.5 million respectively.

## 6. Band-descriptor self-assessment checklist

- **Task Response:** answered all parts? clear position? (Task 1: overview present, key features covered?)
- **Coherence & Cohesion:** logical paragraphs? linking used well (not mechanical)?
- **Lexical Resource:** varied, precise vocabulary? paraphrasing without copying the prompt?
- **Grammatical Range & Accuracy:** mix of simple + complex sentences? few errors that block meaning?

## 🎁 Bonus 1 — Examiner-style self-marking rubric

Score each criterion 0–9, then average for an estimated band. Quick guide:
- **Band 6:** generally relevant, some unclear parts, repetitive linking, noticeable errors.
- **Band 7:** clear position throughout, logical paragraphs, some less-common vocabulary, errors that don't impede meaning.
- **Band 8:** fully developed, well-organised, wide vocabulary used naturally, mostly error-free.

## 🎁 Bonus 2 — Grammar & punctuation fix-list for Band 7

Articles (a/an/the), subject–verb agreement, prepositions, run-on sentences (use full stops), comma splices, plural/uncountable nouns, consistent verb tense, and capital letters. *Fix the 3 you make most often — small errors, when frequent, cap your score.*
