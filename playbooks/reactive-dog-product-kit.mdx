## 1. Before you start (foundations & safety)

- **Reactivity is fear or over-arousal, not "a bad dog."** Goal: change how your dog *feels*, not punish the reaction.
- **Rule out pain first** — ask your vet (pain is a common hidden cause).
- **Management before training** — while you train, *avoid* full-strength triggers (every blow-up sets you back).
- **Force-free gear:** well-fitted **Y-front harness**, **2–3 m long line**, treat pouch, **high-value treats** (tiny, soft). No prong, choke, or e-collars.
- **Threshold** = the distance/intensity where your dog *notices* a trigger but can still eat and disengage. All training happens **under threshold**.
- If your dog has bitten or you feel unsafe: pause and get professional help (§8).

## 2. The 6-week plan (with success markers + troubleshooting)

> *Nota:* ogni settimana ha: obiettivo, 2 esercizi (5–10 min, 1–2x/giorno), "come capire che funziona", e "se sei bloccato".

**Week 1 — Baseline & engagement**
- Goal: rebuild attention in low-trigger places. Exercises: name game / check-in; find threshold distances for the top 1–2 triggers.
- ✅ Success: your dog offers eye contact and takes treats calmly with no trigger present.
- 🔧 Stuck: treats not "high value" enough, or environment too busy — go quieter/closer to home.

**Week 2 — Look At That (engage–disengage)**
- Goal: trigger = "look, then back to me." Exercises: LAT game under threshold; reward disengagement.
- ✅ Success: dog glances at a far trigger and looks back at you for a treat.
- 🔧 Stuck: you're too close — add distance until the glance-and-return is easy.

**Week 3 — Counter-conditioning**
- Goal: "trigger predicts treats." Exercises: open bar / closed bar; treat scatter to lower arousal.
- ✅ Success: dog sees a trigger (far) and looks to you *expecting* food (a good sign the emotion is shifting).
- 🔧 Stuck: timing — treats must start the instant the trigger appears, stop when it's gone.

**Week 4 — Pattern games for walks**
- Goal: tools to move calmly. Exercises: "1-2-3" game; emergency U-turn.
- ✅ Success: you can walk past a known low-level trigger using a pattern game.
- 🔧 Stuck: practice the games with no trigger first so they're automatic.

**Week 5 — Decompression & controlled setups**
- Goal: lower baseline + practice with a helper you control. Exercises: sniffaris/decompression walks; a set-up with a calm helper dog/person at distance.
- ✅ Success: shorter recovery after a sighting; calmer overall on quiet days.
- 🔧 Stuck: too much "trigger stacking" — space out exposures; more rest days.

**Week 6 — Generalize & maintain**
- Goal: real-world short walks + maintenance. Exercises: apply games on low-trigger walks; build your maintenance + "hard day" plan.
- ✅ Success: walks feel predictable; you have a plan for bad days.
- 🔧 Stuck: no progress after ~6–8 weeks → book a force-free pro (§8).

## 7. Quick-reference protocols

- **Under-threshold rule:** train only when your dog can notice the trigger and still eat / look at you. Too close = add distance first.
- **Emergency U-turn (mid-reaction):** cue cheerfully, scatter treats, walk away. **Distance first, train later. Never punish the reaction.**
- **Walk planning:** low-trigger times/routes; always have a bail-out (driveways, side streets).
- **Trigger stacking:** several stressors in a short time = a worse reaction. Spread exposures; add rest days.

## 8. What-to-say scripts

- Approaching person: *"He's in training — please give us space, thank you!"*
- Off-leash dog incoming: *"Please call your dog, mine is not friendly right now!"*
- Curious kids: *"He's working — you can wave from there!"*
- Pushy neighbor: *"We're working on his nerves, so no greetings today — thanks for understanding."*

## When to get professional help

Stop the DIY program and contact a **certified force-free trainer (CSAT, KPA-CTP, CCPDT) or a veterinary behaviorist** if: your dog has **bitten or broken skin**, reactions are **worsening**, you feel **unsafe**, or there's **no progress after ~6–8 weeks**.

## 🎁 Bonus 1 — "Calm at home" decompression & enrichment guide

A calmer dog at home is calmer on walks. Daily: a **settle mat** routine (reward calm), **sniff games** (scatter feeding, snuffle mat), a **long-lasting chew** (licking/chewing lowers arousal), and **less trigger exposure** through windows (frosted film / management). Aim for more sleep — under-rested dogs are more reactive.

## 🎁 Bonus 3 — Force-free gear & treat guide

- **Harness:** well-fitted Y-front (no-pull front clip optional). **Avoid** prong/choke/e-collars.
- **Lead:** 2–3 m long line for freedom under control; a short lead for tight spots.
- **Treats:** tiny, soft, high-value (e.g., cooked chicken, cheese, commercial soft treats) — fast to eat, used a lot.
- **Optional:** a "give us space" leash sleeve, a treat pouch, a snuffle mat.

## 🎁 Bonus 4 — "Hard day reset" + setback script

Bad days happen. **Reset:** skip training, do a sniffari or stay home, give a chew, rest. Tell yourself: *"A setback is data, not failure. We go back a step in distance and rebuild — that's the method working, not breaking."*
