## 1. Before you start (foundations & safety)

> *Nota:* questa sezione protegge te (venditore) e il cane. Tienila all'inizio del PDF.

- **Reactivity is fear or over-arousal, not "a bad dog."** The goal is to change how your dog *feels* about triggers, not to punish the reaction.
- **Rule out pain first.** Ask your vet for a check-up — pain is a common hidden cause.
- **Management comes before training.** While you train, *avoid* full-strength triggers as much as possible (every "blow-up" sets you back).
- **Gear (force-free):** a well-fitted **Y-front harness**, a **2–3 m long line**, a treat pouch, and **high-value treats** (tiny, soft, fast to eat). No prong, choke, or e-collars.
- **Learn the threshold.** Your dog's **threshold** is the distance/intensity at which they *notice* a trigger but can still eat, look at you, and disengage. All training happens **under threshold**.
- **If your dog has bitten or you feel unsafe:** pause this program and get professional help (see §8).

## 2. The 6-week plan

> *Nota:* ogni settimana = obiettivo + 2 esercizi + "win" da segnare. 5–10 minuti, 1–2 volte al giorno. Niente fretta: si va al ritmo del cane.

**Week 1 — Baseline & engagement**
- Goal: rebuild attention on you in low-trigger places.
- Exercises: (a) **Name game / check-in** — reward your dog for voluntarily looking at you. (b) **Find your threshold distance** for the top 1–2 triggers and write it in the tracker.

**Week 2 — Look At That (engage–disengage)**
- Goal: trigger = "look, then back to me."
- Exercises: (a) **LAT game** — the instant your dog notices a trigger *under threshold*, mark ("yes") and treat; they learn to look and disengage. (b) Practice **disengagement** — reward turning away from a trigger.

**Week 3 — Counter-conditioning**
- Goal: "trigger predicts treats" → the emotional flip.
- Exercises: (a) **Open bar / closed bar** — trigger appears (under threshold) → treats rain; trigger gone → treats stop. (b) **Treat scatter** to lower arousal after a sighting.

**Week 4 — Pattern games for walks**
- Goal: tools to move *through* the world calmly.
- Exercises: (a) **"1-2-3" game** — count and treat on "3"; predictable rhythm lowers stress. (b) **Emergency U-turn** — a happy cue to turn and leave when a trigger is too close.

**Week 5 — Decompression & controlled setups**
- Goal: lower the baseline + practice with a *helper* trigger you control.
- Exercises: (a) **Sniffaris / decompression walks** in quiet places (sniffing lowers stress). (b) A **set-up session** with a calm helper dog/person at a safe distance, decreasing distance only when your dog stays relaxed.

**Week 6 — Generalize & maintain**
- Goal: real-world walks + a plan to keep going.
- Exercises: (a) Apply the games on **short real walks** at low-trigger times. (b) Build your **maintenance routine** and your "hard day" plan. Reassess thresholds; if stuck, book a force-free pro.

## 7. Quick-reference protocols

**The under-threshold rule**
> Train only when your dog can *notice the trigger and still eat / look at you*. Too close = move away first.

**The emergency U-turn (mid-reaction)**
> If a trigger appears too close: cheerfully say your U-turn cue, scatter a few treats on the ground, and walk the other way. **Get distance first, train later.** Never punish the reaction.

**Walk planning**
> Choose **low-trigger times and routes** (early morning, quiet streets). Keep a "bail-out" plan (driveways, side streets) for every walk.

**What-to-say scripts (people & dog owners)**
- Approaching person: *"He's in training — please give us space, thank you!"*
- Off-leash dog running over: *"Please call your dog, mine is not friendly right now!"*
- Curious kids: *"He's working, you can wave from there!"*
- A "give us space" lead/harness slip + a calm, confident U-turn does most of the work.

## 8. When to get professional help (importante)

Stop the DIY program and contact a **certified force-free trainer (CSAT, KPA-CTP, CCPDT) or a veterinary behaviorist** if: your dog has **bitten or broken skin**, reactions are getting **worse**, you feel **unsafe**, or there is **no progress after ~6–8 weeks**. This product is a starting framework, not a replacement for hands-on help.
